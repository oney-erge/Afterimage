# Lifecycle boundaries

- `afterimage.server.jobs.JobRegistry` owns in-process jobs and their lane admission. `ModelRegistry` persists job/model rows. On server restart, worker-dependent job states, including paused jobs, become `interrupted`; a retry is a new job.
- UI chat and research jobs use the `gpu` lane. The OpenAI-compatible chat endpoint and engine unload/removal use that same lane. A streaming producer holds it until generation and statistics collection end, even when its client stops reading. GPU ownership is process-local; multiple server processes are not coordinated.
- Cancellation is cooperative. `cancelled` is the accepted user-facing outcome; `cleanup_pending` remains true while the worker exits. Job state transitions and their durable updates are serialized per job, so a committed completion or error wins over a later cancel, and an accepted cancellation cannot be overwritten by a late result or progress callback. The same model cannot be retried or removed during cleanup. Model-lifecycle work also holds a per-model writer lock; unrelated downloads may proceed after cancellation releases the public lane.
- `pause_requested` represents accepted intent until the worker reports a pause at a checkpoint. Progress alone cannot establish that execution has paused, and stale pause/resume callbacks do not reverse a newer control decision.
- The engine cache owns one loaded target/draft and keys the target by the complete engine configuration fingerprint. An active GPU lane protects mutation, eviction, and unload. Downloaded files, prepared stores, and runnable models remain distinct states.

Inspect `GET /api/jobs` or `GET /api/jobs/{id}` for progress and cleanup state. Interrupted records may be dismissed; restart does not imply a paused thread can resume. See `tests/test_model_registry.py`, `tests/test_job_deletion.py`, and `tests/test_generation_admission.py` for restart, writer, and admission coverage. Live model/GPU behavior must still be verified on the target hardware.
